## 3. Declared licence and upstream/source licences

- **OSF node licence: none declared.** The API returns `node_license: null` and
  the node lists no licence file; `public: true`.
- **Paper licence:** arXiv.org perpetual non-exclusive licence (covers the
  paper, not the dataset).
- **Upstream content:** the transcripts are original to this study. The AI
  witness turns were generated by third-party services (OpenAI GPT-4.5 /
  GPT-4o, Meta Llama-3.1-405B via Together, GPT-5 in the 15-minute study, and a
  local ELIZA). Those providers' terms attach to the *generated text* in the
  corpus and are a separate, unresolved question (see §13).
- **Derived files inside the release:** `data/reason_classifications_4o.csv`
  and `data/strategy_classifications_4o.csv` are GPT-4o classifications of
  participant free text produced by the original authors.

**Consequence:** a public repository plus a consent statement covering public
sharing is *permission to access*, not an explicit grant of rights to
redistribute or to train on. This record therefore treats the absence of a
declared licence as a restriction, not as a default-open state.

## 4. Permission to modify and create derivatives

Not explicitly granted anywhere in the release. **Project policy adopted:**
derivatives (canonical normalisation, splits, feature tables) may be created and
kept **local**, with provenance stamped per `v2/experiment_design.md` §9. The
immutable copy under `v2/data/sources/` is never edited.

## 5. Permission to redistribute original and transformed text

Not explicitly granted. **Project policy adopted:** do **not** publish
transcript text, message content, or free-text participant responses — including
inside derived `v2/data/canonical/` or `v2/data/sft/` artefacts, quotations in
write-ups beyond short illustrative fragments, or model-training corpora that
would be released. Publish scripts, hashes, provenance, and aggregate numbers
instead. This is the §8.2 rule for the unclear-redistribution case.

## 6. Research-only, non-commercial, or attribution restrictions

None stated as licence terms. The consent language (§7) is framed around
*analysis* and *public sharing*, which supports academic research use and does
not obviously extend to commercial use or to model training. Treat commercial
use as out of scope for this project.

Anonymization, as stated in `15_mins/codebook.txt` for that study: message

profile table". The same placeholder scheme is present in the 2025 release.

**Measured**, not quoted (`v2/results/stage_a/data_inspection.md`): in the main
study 431 of 18,058 messages carry `is_changed = TRUE`, and 404 messages contain
an anonymization placeholder — `<NAME>` ×362, `<LOCATION>` ×55, `<USERNAME>` ×7,

witnesses, where they are prompt context rather than a real person's details.

Note that the anonymization was itself performed by an LLM, so it is a
best-effort screen and not a guarantee.

## 8. PII or sensitive-content risks

Measured screens (details and counts in the inspection report):

- **Two residual re-identifiers found, one in each study — REVIEWED 2026-08-17,
  handling decision in §8.1 below.** Both are in `tt_profile.other`, a free-text
  column that the codebook's "stripped PII columns" step does not cover.
  - *Main study,* 1 row (47 characters): contains a standalone 24-character hex
    token, i.e. Prolific-worker-ID shaped.
  - *15-minute study,* 1 row (334 characters): contains a 24-character hex token
    **and** an e-mail address at `email.prolific.com` whose local part is
    byte-identical to that same token. That is Prolific's participant relay
    address, so this row is simultaneously a cross-study re-identifier and a
    working contact channel for the participant. The Stage A inspection recorded
    this row only as "trips the e-mail screen", which understated it; the
    inspection report's §8 item 2 is superseded by this entry.
- Two message bodies trip a phone-number-shaped screen; both are false positives
  on inspection (a date range and a counting sequence).
- Demographics present for 307/323 profiles (gender, year of birth, education,
  chatbot-use frequency, LLM familiarity) — pseudonymous but re-identifying in
  combination, and to be kept out of anything published.
- Conversation content is adversarial small talk with participants discussing
  personal details; no systematic screen for sensitive topics has been run.

### 8.1 Handling decision for `tt_profile.other` (settled 2026-08-17)

**Decision: `tt_profile.other` is EXCLUDED from every derived artefact, in both
studies, unconditionally and permanently — not "pending review".** The column is
dropped at the canonical-loader boundary rather than filtered downstream, so no
later stage can reintroduce it by default.

| Question | Decision |
|---|---|
| Read into `v2/data/canonical/` | **No.** Dropped by the loader; a test asserts its absence. |
| Used as a model feature | **No.** It is a free-text "anything else?" box with no analytic role in Track A. |
| Quoted in write-ups, even partially | **No**, including the redacted-looking fragments. |
| Values reproduced in this repo | **No.** They are characterised structurally (length, charset, format match) and never printed. `v2/scripts/` contains no script that emits them. |
| The two affected participants' other columns | **Retained.** The re-identifier is confined to this one column; excluding the rows entirely would silently bias the demographic tables for no added protection. |
| Notify the authors | **Yes.** Drafted at `v2/results/stage_a/author_contact_drafts.md` (email B), not yet sent. Record the outcome in §14 item 5. |

**Why exclusion rather than redaction.** Redacting the two known values would
leave the column in the pipeline while implying it had been cleaned. The screens
here are regex flags over 383 free-text responses, not a guarantee — the two hits
were found by patterns chosen in advance, and a worker id written with spaces or
a contact detail in prose would pass them. Excluding the whole column is the only
action whose correctness does not depend on the screen's recall.

**Scope limit, stated plainly.** This decision covers `tt_profile.other` only. It
does not certify the other free-text columns (`strategy`, `strategy_change`,
`expt_aware_details`, `verdict.reason`, and message content), which passed the
same imperfect screens and have not had a systematic sensitive-content pass
(§14 item 6).

## 9. Intended use and mismatch with this project

**Author-intended use:** measuring whether humans can distinguish LLMs from
humans in a controlled three-party Turing test, and analysing interrogator
strategies and stated reasons.

**Our use:** Track A — passive human-vs-AI identity judgment by *models*, with
calibration, template normalisation, and limited source/family holdout.

**Mismatches that constrain claims** (also recorded in the inspection report):

1. The human class is participants *instructed to convince an interrogator they
   are human, under adversarial questioning*. It is not ordinary conversation.
2. The interrogator side is a real, adaptive human policy, but the corpus is
   fixed: it cannot answer counterfactual "what if a different question were
   asked" questions. This is exactly why v2.1's active track lives in D0.
3. There is no turn-level belief, confidence, or ledger of any kind — one
   verdict and one confidence per game, at the end.
4. Substantive AI families are few (OpenAI, Meta-Llama, plus ELIZA as a weak
   control), so family holdout is exploratory at best.

## 10. Author-stated encouraged or discouraged uses (beyond licence terms)

- Jones & Bergen state the data is shared for analysis; no statement was found
  encouraging or discouraging model training on it. **Absence of a statement is
  not permission.**
- **Inverse Turing Bench** (Hager, Rathi, Hasan, and Jones — an author of the
  source study — arXiv:2606.21844, CC BY 4.0) builds a benchmark of 557 dialogue
  pairs from this corpus and states, verbatim: *"Risks of this study include use
  of the benchmark or its dataset for training LLMs to be less detectable; to
  adapt the benchmark for such future challenges, we would re-release with more
  complex dialogues."*
  Its limitations section additionally notes that the benchmark "may not account
  for detection of more recent models, or other model families", that judges
  "often use adversarial questioning to detect AI, so transcripts may contain
  more relevant information for detection than unsuspecting Human-AI
  conversations", and that there is a "lack of diversity in the AI witness
  persona".
- **Read as a caution that binds this project:** an author of the source data,
  writing about a derivative of exactly these transcripts, singles out training
  on them as the named risk. V2.1 therefore treats the corpus as an
  **evaluation** resource by default. It is also a reproduction target
  (arm A1), which is the use the benchmark was published for.

## 11. Dual-use risk, including whether training could improve concealment

- **Direct risk.** These transcripts pair each AI witness turn with a human
  interrogator's adversarial probe and a labelled outcome (`is_correct`,
  `confidence`). That is a ready-made signal for training a model to *pass* —
  the concealment direction the ITB authors warn about. V2.1's investigator-side
  use is the opposite direction (detection), but a detector trained on this data
  is also a reward model for a concealer, so the artefact is dual-use regardless
  of our intent.
- **Mitigations adopted:** (a) no adapter is trained on this corpus in v2.1 —
  the only required SFT is on D0 synthetic data; (b) Track A's trained component
  is a lightweight classifier head on a frozen, temporally clean representation
  model, whose weights are of little use for generation; (c) no transcript text
  is published (§5); (d) any future proposal to train a *generator* on this data
  requires a new registry decision and author contact, and is presumed refused.
- **Residual risk accepted:** publishing which cues discriminate humans from AI
  is itself mildly concealment-enabling. This is inherent to the research
  question and is shared with the published literature.

## 14. Unresolved questions

1. **No declared licence on the OSF node.** Ask the contact (`cameron@ucsd.edu`)
   for an explicit licence, and specifically whether model *training* on the
   transcripts is acceptable to the authors. Until answered, §12 stands.
2. **Provider terms on generated text.** OpenAI/Meta/Together terms may attach to
   the AI witness turns; unexamined. Relevant only if training or redistribution
   is ever proposed.
3. **The 15-minute study has no accompanying paper found.** Its consent, IRB, and
   preregistration status are inferred from the shared paradigm and its codebook,
   not from a document naming it. **Do not assume the 2025 consent statement
   covers it**; confirm before using it for anything beyond inspection.
4. **The published analysis subset is not reproducible from the release by the
   stated criteria alone.** The release holds 1,140 completed games; the paper
   analyses 1,023. Naive application of the stated exclusions gives 985–1,014
   (measured). Reproduce with the released `.Rmd` scripts before any comparison
   with published numbers.
5. **Residual PII — handling settled, disclosure OPEN.** The two `tt_profile.other`
   re-identifiers (§8) are reviewed and the column is permanently excluded from
   derived artefacts (§8.1). What remains open is **notifying the authors**: a
   draft is held at `v2/results/stage_a/author_contact_drafts.md` (email B) and
   has **not been sent**. Record the send date and any reply here. This is the
   only §14 item whose *internal* handling is closed.
6. **Anonymization was LLM-performed**, so recall is unknown. If any derived
   artefact were ever proposed for release, a fresh PII pass would be required.

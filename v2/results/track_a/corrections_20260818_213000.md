## C1 — "the ceiling is the corpus's" is an overclaim. Narrowed.

**Where:** `a2_frozen_rep_20260818_writeup.md` §0 and §5 ("Whose ceiling is it.
It is the corpus's, not bag-of-words'"); `v2/DECISIONS.md` 2026-08-18 A2 entry
title and body; `track_a_a2.py` module docstring (corrected in code).

**What was claimed:** that the persona ceiling is a property of the corpus rather
than of bag-of-words.

**What the evidence supports:** *two methods — a TF-IDF baseline and one frozen
Qwen2.5-7B representation with a logistic head — both fail to transfer between
these two prompt families, and the neural one fails harder.*

**Why the original is too strong.** "The corpus's ceiling" is an impossibility
claim about all methods. It rests on n = 2 methods, and the second one carries
three specific handicaps that a stronger A2 would not: crude mean-pooling as a
sentence representation, a 512-dimension random projection (a compute
compromise), and a single untuned head at one regularisation setting. A1 (Inverse
Turing Bench reproduction) and A3 (turn-wise evaluation) were never run. A
different representation, a nested calibrator, or turn-wise evidence could all in
principle recover signal this pair missed.

**What remains true and is the useful finding:** the failure is *not* an artefact
of lexical representation, since swapping the representation entirely did not
help — it hurt. That is strong evidence of severe prompt dependence in this
corpus, and it is sufficient to justify stopping Track A. It is not proof that no
learnable general signal exists here.

**Corrected wording to quote going forward:**

> Both a bag-of-words baseline and a frozen-representation head fail to transfer
> across persona prompt families on this corpus, indicating severe prompt
> dependence. Whether *any* method could transfer is untested.

## C2 — A2 is not "calibrated". It is a probabilistic head evaluated for calibration.

**Where:** `a2_frozen_rep_20260818_writeup.md` §0 and §5 ("a calibrated head over
a frozen ... representation"); `v2/DECISIONS.md` A2 entry; `track_a_a2.py`
(corrected in code, this session).

**The distinction:** `track_a_a2.py` fits an ordinary L2 logistic regression and
*measures* Brier, ECE and reliability on out-of-fold predictions. It fits **no**
Platt, isotonic, or temperature calibrator inside the training folds.
**Out-of-fold calibration evaluation is not out-of-fold calibration.**

**Consequences, stated precisely:**

- The reported numbers are **legitimate measurements** and stand unchanged:
  people-only dialogue Brier 0.1663, ECE 0.1008; persona holdout ECE 0.283 and
  0.364. They describe how well this head's raw probabilities happen to be
  calibrated.
- They are **not** evidence that a calibration procedure was applied.
- Design §13.1 specifies a "calibrated full-history classifier head", so what was
  built is a **deviation from the plan**, now recorded as such rather than
  presented as compliance.
- The comparison "A2's ECE 0.101 is better than A0's 0.173" remains valid — both
  are uncalibrated estimators measured the same way.

**No calibrator was added.** Building one is deliberately deferred; the review
asked for the description to be corrected first, and fitting a calibrator now
would be new experimental work on a concluded track.

## C3 — Stage B / Track A is CONCLUDED EARLY, not "complete"

**Where:** the earlier writeups and `STATUS.md` described Track A as complete.

Against the frozen plan (§13.1), the arm inventory is:

| arm | plan | actual |
|---|---|---|
| A0 majority/random/lexical/statistical | required | **done** |
| A1 Inverse Turing Bench reproduction | required | **SKIPPED — never implemented** |
| A2 calibrated head on frozen clean representation | required | **done, uncalibrated** (C2) |
| A3 same estimator, turn-wise | required | **DEFERRED — never implemented** |

"Complete" implies the planned arms ran. Two did not. The accurate description is
**concluded early as a consequence of the stopping decision**, with A1 skipped and
A3 deferred to optional Stage D.

A1's non-implementation was declared up front (`a0_baselines_20260818_writeup.md`
§1) on the grounds that its 557 games are 48.9% of the corpus and biased long, and
that the A2-vs-A1 contrast had been demoted. That reasoning stands. What was
missing is the bookkeeping consequence: an arm declined for good reasons is still
an arm not run, and Stage B cannot be called complete while it is outstanding.

## C4 — the Gate 1 verdict, restated at the correct width

Superseding the verdict paragraph in `a2_frozen_rep_20260818_writeup.md` §5:

> **Broad Gate 1 — failed or unavailable.** Gate 1 requires "useful out-of-source
> discrimination and calibration". No out-of-*source* holdout was ever available:
> the only other real corpus in hand is the 15-minute study, whose Gate 0 is
> unresolved, so it was never read. The strongest holdouts actually run were
> within-corpus — held-out people, held-out recruitment population, held-out
> witness system, held-out persona prompt — and the persona holdout failed for
> both methods tried. Broad Gate 1 is therefore **not passed**, partly by failure
> and partly by unavailability, and the two causes must not be conflated.
>
> **Within-configuration passive detection — established.** Against *seen*
> respondent configurations the signal is real and well characterised: A0 0.960,
> A2 0.866 [0.842, 0.889] paired accuracy, A2 with out-of-fold ECE 0.101 and
> two-sided recognition (74.2% of AI dialogues flagged, 80.4% of humans cleared).
> It survives removal of interrogator text, reply-rate/length, redaction
> placeholders, and respondent silence, and it transfers between recruitment
> populations.
>
> **A1 skipped** as a consequence of the stopping decision. **A3 deferred** to
> optional Stage D, if that is ever run.
>
> **Not established:** detection against unseen prompt or model configurations;
> detection against an adversary free to choose its own prompt; any claim that no
> method could do better on this corpus.

**The internal contradiction this resolves.** `v2/DECISIONS.md` (2026-08-18,
rung-4 entry) records persona and family rungs as **non-gating** — "reported but
not gating" per the Gate 1 ladder. The next entry then used persona failure to
conclude Track A. Both cannot stand as written.

**Resolution:** the stopping decision was a **judgement call informed by a
non-gating rung, not a gate firing.** Persona failure did not automatically fail
Gate 1; it made further Track A investment unattractive, because the axis that
failed is the one v2.1's question depends on. That is a legitimate reason to stop
and a legitimate reason to record a plan revision — but it is a revision, not the
plan executing as written. Recorded as such in `v2/DECISIONS.md`.
